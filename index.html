inbox-jev

Every ten minutes it labels your new mail with Jev, archives what has nothing for you to do and unsubscribes you from pitches it can verify.

The inbox, after one pass

Only mail that needs you stays in the inbox

Each email gets one of eight labels. Personal mail, and automated mail that asks you to do something, stay in the inbox. Receipts, notifications, newsletters, promotions and sales pitches are archived. When Jev isn't sure enough, the email stays where it is with Jev/Review and its best guess.

The pass itself

A pass runs every ten minutes on your own machine

A launchd or systemd timer starts it. Each email gets a category, a confidence and an action, and the decision goes into a SQLite database so you can look it up later or undo it.

Playground

Try it on an email(live)

Paste an email, or take a sample, and the published package decides it here the way a pass would: the headers' signals, what Jev gets, Jev's answer and the policy's verdict, for an inbox with no rules and the default categories. What you paste goes to a function on this site and on to TypeSafe under the author's key, and is not stored. Each visitor gets fifty cents of Jev, a few thousand emails.

How it decides

How it decides(8)

Every email goes through the same steps. The eight in the figure are worked examples from the spec, and each one is also a test that runs the real code. Pick one and follow it to what Gmail shows afterwards.

Your rules first

Your rules decide first

An allowlisted sender, or a sender with a rule, is decided without asking Jev. Rules come from your corrections, from rules set, or appear on their own after Jev has answered the same category three times at 0.95 or more.

Then Jev

Jev reads the headers and the first lines

It also gets the domains the links point to, Gmail's own SPF, DKIM and DMARC verdicts, and whether you have ever written to the sender. It never gets a full URL, and the body is never stored. It answers with one category and how sure it is.

Then policy

The confidence picks the action

If it reaches the category's bar, that category's action runs. If several archive categories add up to 0.90, the email is archived under the strongest of them. Anything else stays in the inbox with Jev/Review.

What it does

What it does to your inbox(6)

Labels

One Gmail label per category

Eight labels under the Jev/ prefix, plus Jev/Needs Reply, Jev/Invoices and Jev/Review. Rename them or add a category in categories.yaml.

Archive

Archives what has nothing for you to do

Receipts, notifications, newsletters, promotions and pitches leave the inbox once Jev is at least 0.80 sure. Below the bar they stay, with Jev/Review.

Unsubscribe

Unsubscribes only from senders it can verify

Marketing and cold outreach at 0.90 or more, from a sender that passes DMARC and offers a List-Unsubscribe header, never a group or anyone you wrote to.

Corrections

Learns from your corrections

Swap a label or move an email back and the next pass writes a rule for that sender. Three answers at 0.95 or more make one on their own.

Digest

One Slack message every morning

At 08:00: what needs you, what it archived, which lists it unsubscribed you from and what it learned. Short enough for a phone.

Undo

Everything it does can be undone

Every decision is in SQLite. Watch it in dry_run, then label, before act moves anything. One command brings a day's mail back.

Categories

Categories(8)

These are the defaults the package ships with, and they are one person's preferences. You can change the criteria, the bars and the actions, or add a category, in your own copy of categories.yaml.

categories.yaml
>_[ categories.yaml ]≡
# categories.yaml: the defaults, yours to edit
categories:
  - key: marketing
    label: Jev/Marketing
    criteria: >-
      Promotional email from a legitimate company:
      ads, sales, discounts, product launches,
      webinars, event promotions, upsells.
    action: archive_unsubscribe
    min_confidence: 0.90
accounts.yaml
>_[ accounts.yaml ]≡
# accounts.yaml
accounts:
  - email: you@example.com
    mode: act
    act_categories: [marketing, newsletter, notification]
    allow_domains: [example.com, client.example]
    allow_senders: [team@partner.example]
    decide_each_email: [notify@crm.example]
    slack_user_id: U0123ABCD
    digest_language: es
KeepJev/ImportantNo bar
Actionstays in the inbox

Written by a real person specifically to you: clients, teammates, partners, investors, candidates, decisions and questions addressed to you. It is not mass mail that only greets you by name. Allowlisted senders and emails you pull back to the inbox land here.

KeepJev/ActionNo bar
Actionstays in the inbox

Automated email that needs you to do something: approve a payment, update an expiring card, verify a domain, sign a document, fix something before a deadline. Also a lead from your own website's form, a meeting someone booked with you, and a calendar invitation waiting on your answer.

ArchiveJev/TransactionalBar 0.80
Actionarchived

The record of something you did or bought, with nothing left to do: receipts, paid invoices, order and booking confirmations, shipping updates, verification codes, copies of documents you already signed.

ArchiveJev/NotificationsBar 0.80
Actionarchived

FYI alerts and digests from the tools you use: GitHub, Slack, Calendar, Drive, Jira, Linear, analytics, monitoring, CI. Reminders of meetings already in your calendar and plain "Accepted" replies to invitations you sent. There is nothing for you to do with them.

ArchiveJev/NewslettersBar 0.80
Actionarchived, never unsubscribed

Editorial content you subscribed to: newsletters, digests, blog updates, industry news. It informs rather than sells.

Archive + unsubJev/MarketingBar 0.90
Actionarchived and unsubscribed

Promotional email from a legitimate company: ads, sales, discounts, launches, webinars, event promotions, upsells. Unsubscribed only when the sender passes DMARC and offers a List-Unsubscribe header.

Archive + unsubJev/OutreachBar 0.90
Actionarchived and unsubscribed, never from a known contact

An unsolicited pitch from someone you have no relationship with, written to look personal: agencies, lead-gen, recruiting, "quick question" sequences, podcast and sponsorship invitations.

SpamJev/SpamBar 0.90
Actionmoved to Gmail's Spam

Scams and phishing: a reply that answers no thread, links to domains unrelated to the sender, a conversation between other people pasted into a message to you, an authentication pass that proves a domain other than the one it claims. Below 0.90 it stays in the inbox with Jev/Review, already labeled, for you to confirm.

Questions

Before you let it touch your mail(6)

What does it never do?

It never deletes an email. It unsubscribes only when the sender passes DMARC and the email has a List-Unsubscribe header, and even then only when the sender is not a group or a discussion list, you have never written to them, Jev is at 0.90 or more and the account is in act mode. Starred mail, mail you moved back and mail whose labels you edited by hand are left alone. Below a category's bar it does nothing that would need undoing.

What does it send to Jev?

The headers that matter, the first characters of the body, the domains the links point to without the rest of the URL, Gmail's SPF, DKIM and DMARC verdicts, and whether you have ever written to the sender. The database and the log keep the sender, the subject and the classification, and never the body. The search of your sent mail reads only the To, Cc and Bcc fields.

How do I start without risk?

Run it in dry_run for a week. Every decision is recorded and the digest tells you what it would have done, but nothing in Gmail changes and no rule is learned. Switch to label when the categories look right, and to act when the labels do, for every category at once or one at a time with act_categories.

What if it gets one wrong?

Fix it in Gmail. Swap the label, remove Jev/Review or move the email back to the inbox, and the next pass records the correction and a rule for that sender. Tomorrow's digest lists what it learned. To undo a whole day, inbox-jev restore --since 1d moves everything archived or spammed back and removes its labels. An unsubscribe can't be undone, which is why it has the longest list of conditions.

Where does it run?

On your Mac or Linux machine, as a launchd or systemd user timer that inbox-jev schedule install sets up, with a pass every ten minutes and the digest at 08:00. It can also run on a server with Docker and systemd timers, with a watchdog for stale runs, nightly backups and Slack alerts. Run one copy per account.

Why trust the rules?

Every rule is written down twice. The functional spec has 50 worked examples, and each one is a test that runs the real code. The 53 architecture decision records say why each rule is the way it is and which alternatives were rejected. A change to how it decides is checked against real mail before it is merged.

Install

Install it in two commands

It is free, MIT licensed, and runs on your own machine. You need a TypeSafe API key and a Google OAuth client of your own, and the setup wizard walks you through both. Slack is optional. Your email goes to Google and to TypeSafe, and to no other server.

StarForkIssues

Python 3.11 or newer, uv or pipx

The first command installs the tool. The second asks for your TypeSafe key, walks you through a Google OAuth client of your own, authorizes your Gmail and offers a first dry run. Then inbox-jev schedule install runs it every ten minutes.

uv tool install inbox-jev && inbox-jev init

If you run it from a checkout of the repo, it uses the checkout. Run it on a server with Docker
