How it decidesFig. 3

How it decides(8)

Every email takes the same path. Pick one of the eight in the figure, all taken from the worked examples the test suite runs against the real code, and follow it to what Gmail shows you afterwards.

Your rules first

Decided without a call

The allowlist and the sender rules decide on the spot. Rules come from your corrections, from rules set, or on their own after three answers at 0.95 or more.

Then Jev

Headers, first lines, signals

The domains links point to, Gmail's SPF, DKIM and DMARC verdicts, and whether you ever wrote to the sender. Never a URL, never a stored body. Jev answers one category and how sure it is.

Then policy

Confidence becomes an action

At or above the category's bar, its action. Archive categories that together reach 0.90, archived. Anything else stays in the inbox with Jev/Review and nothing destructive happens.

Archive

Archives what can wait

Receipts, notifications, newsletters, promos and pitches leave the inbox once Jev is at least 0.80 sure, 0.90 where it also unsubscribes. Below that the email stays, with Jev/Review and the suggested label.

Unsubscribe

Unsubscribes only from senders it can verify

Only marketing and cold outreach at 0.90 or more, from a sender with a DMARC pass and a List-Unsubscribe header, not a group, not anyone you ever wrote to, in act mode. One-click per RFC 8058, else a mailto:, else a link in your digest.

Corrections

Learns from one swapped label

Swap a label, remove Jev/Review or move an archived email back: the next run records a correction and a rule for that sender. Three consistent answers at 0.95 or more make a rule on their own. Starred mail is never touched.

Digest

A digest, not a dashboard

One Slack message every morning, in English or Spanish: how many emails need you and which, what Jev did, who you were unsubscribed from and what it learned from your fixes. Readable on a phone in a few seconds.

Undo

Every action recorded, every action undoable

Each decision lands in SQLite with its confidence and probabilities. Three modes let you watch for a week before anything moves: dry_run records, label only labels, act acts. One command moves a day's archived mail back.

Questions

Before you let it touch your mail(6)

What does it never do?

It never deletes anything. It never unsubscribes without a DMARC pass and a List-Unsubscribe header, never through a group or a discussion list, never from someone you have written to, never below 0.90, and never in dry_run or label mode. It never archives starred mail, mail you restored, or mail whose labels you edited by hand. Below a category's bar nothing destructive happens at all.

What does it send to Jev?

The headers that matter, the first characters of the body, the domains the links point to (never a URL), Gmail's SPF, DKIM and DMARC verdicts, and whether you ever wrote to the sender. Bodies are never stored in the database or logged: only sender, subject and the classification. The sent-mail search reads To, Cc and Bcc of your sent emails, never their text.

How do I start without risk?

Run dry_run for a week: every decision is recorded and the digest tells you what it would have done, while nothing in Gmail changes and no rule is learned. Switch to label when the categories look right, and to act when the labels do, all at once or category by category with act_categories.

What if it gets one wrong?

Fix it in Gmail: swap the label, remove Jev/Review, or move the email back to the inbox. The next run records the correction and a rule for that sender, and tomorrow's digest lists what it learned. To undo a day, inbox-jev restore --since 1d moves everything archived or spammed back and forgets its labels. An unsubscribe can't be undone, which is why it has the longest list of conditions.

Where does it run?

On your Mac or Linux machine, as a launchd or systemd user timer: a triage pass every ten minutes and the digest at 08:00, installed with inbox-jev schedule install. Or on a server with Docker and systemd timers, with a staleness watchdog, nightly backups and Slack alerts. One copy per account, never two.

Why trust the rules?

Every rule is written down twice: as a functional spec with 50 worked examples, each one a test that runs the real code, and as 53 architecture decision records that keep the alternatives that were rejected. A change to how it decides is checked against real mail before it merges.

InstallFig. 12

Install it in two commands

Free, MIT-licensed, yours to run. You bring a TypeSafe API key and a Google OAuth client of your own, the wizard walks you through both, Slack is optional. Nothing of yours passes through anyone's server but Google's and TypeSafe's.

StarForkIssues

Python 3.11 or newer, uv or pipx

The first command installs the tool. The second asks for your TypeSafe key, walks you through a Google OAuth client of your own, authorizes your Gmail and offers a first dry run. Then inbox-jev schedule install runs it every ten minutes.

uv tool install inbox-jev && inbox-jev init

Run from a checkout of the repo, it uses the checkout instead. Run it on a server with Docker
